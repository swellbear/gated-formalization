## 1. Plain-language summary

This application tested a strong public claim about U.S. debt-limit votes: that any increase or suspension of the debt limit **must** come with **equal** spending cuts over the next decade, and that without that balance such increases are **fiscally irresponsible** and **should not be enacted**.

What held up is mainly structural and modest. Raising or suspending the debt limit is not itself a new spending appropriation; pairing cuts with a limit vote is a procedural/political choice, not a financing identity. Under a locked, official scorekeeping package (P-Score-Strict+R2), a stipulated “balance” test can be stated clearly. Some historical episodes (FRA 2023, BCA 2011) show that Congress has sometimes paired limit action with CBO-scored discretionary savings — as **analogues only**, not as proof of the original slogan.

What did **not** hold up is the persuasive package as written. Unrestricted “must,” bare “equal,” bare “spending cuts,” unrestricted “fiscally irresponsible,” and “should not be enacted” could not be tested in non-derivative form (forced-deviation terms FD1–FD5). No public instance cleared full headroom equality under the lock. Soft or hard enactment bans were never forced by the admitted descriptive layers.

**Bottom line:** The original claim is **not** well-constrained. The run closed **Stable Provisional**. A later successor application cleaned the wording into a soft scorekept pairing recommendation; that successor does **not** salvage or overwrite this parent claim.

## 3. How it was examined

The work followed the repo’s two-phase gated method.

**Phase 1** diagnosed what the claim actually requires. Early admitted layers separated debt-limit financing from new spending (L1a), and recorded that “equal,” “spending cuts,” and “fiscally irresponsible” are under-determined without locks (L1b–d). Enactment (“should not”) was not forced by descriptive premises (L2a). Default/disruption tradeoffs were noted as relevant if pairing is binding, with magnitudes still open (L2b).

Because G2/G3 (what “equal spending cuts” means) blocked progress, the method ran **locking-scaffolding**. Every realistic package scored **Moderate deviation or higher** relative to the original wording — **no Minimal-deviation package**. That triggered mandatory **forced-deviation extraction** (FD1–FD5). The operator then selected **P-Score-Strict + R2**: CBO-style scored non-interest discretionary+mandatory outlay reductions, current-law baseline, anti-gimmick, matched to estimated borrowing headroom from the increase **or suspension**.

Under that lock only, the balance test became well-posed (L3a) and a stipulated “irresponsible-*under-package*” label became available (L3b) — explicitly **not** the claim’s bare evaluative verdict. Empirical instance search for C ≥ H under the lock was treated as open / currently intractable (L3c); enactment still not forced (L3d).

**Evidence passes** admitted narrow H.R.10078 design overlap (L4a), rejected clean full-instance equality (L4b, L4d), and admitted FRA 2023 / BCA 2011 as historical **analogues** only (L4c).

Closeout kept original wording by default as Stable Provisional. Post-closeout, the operator requested revision; scaffolding produced Rank 1 and opened the successor application.

**Method path:** Phase 1 → forced-deviation + G2/G3 scaffolding → P-Score-Strict+R2 lock → two evidence passes → Original-Claim Assessment closeout → (post-closeout) Claim-Revision → Rank 1 successor.
