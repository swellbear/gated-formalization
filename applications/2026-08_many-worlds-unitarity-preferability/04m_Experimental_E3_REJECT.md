## 1. Rigid separation

### Pure unitary consequences (Schrödinger + already-admitted layers only)
- A closed system’s state \(\lvert\Psi(t)\rangle\) evolves by a unitary group generated by a Hamiltonian (L₀).
- For any tensor factorization and any orthonormal basis of a subsystem, one can write Schmidt / relative-state decompositions of \(\lvert\Psi\rangle\) (linear algebra on Hilbert space).
- Under L1c, Everettian programs aim not to add a collapse or Bohmian guidance **law**.

### Added structure or idealizations (everything else)
- Privileging some decompositions as “the” relative states that correspond to experiences / records / branches.
- Treating “no unique preferred basis needed” as solving the preferred-basis **problem** (which asks which decomposition is physically/experientially relevant).
- Any rule connecting relative-state formalism to quasi-classical worlds or measurement records.
- Ignoring that without further structure, every basis yields a decomposition (IEP/SEP preferred-basis problem).

**Claim of E3:** Because relative states exist in every basis as a unitary/linear-algebra fact, A1 closes: no preferred-basis postulate is required.

## 3. Red-team pass (before admission)

Strongest objections:
1. **Redefinition:** A1 was whether decoherence/branching *packaging* is extra structure for physically relevant worlds—not whether relative states exist formally in every basis.
2. **Hidden structure:** Without selecting a basis, there is no fact about which worlds/records obtain (standard preferred-basis problem statement).
3. **Does not constrain L2a:** Leaves the decoherence-vs-extra fork untouched; only denies that a *postulate named “preferred basis”* is needed by renaming the problem away.
4. **Circularity with experience:** Any move from “relative states exist” to “observers see definite outcomes” reintroduces selection structure.
