## 1. Rigid separation

### Pure unitary consequences (Schrödinger + already-admitted layers only)
- Unitary evolution preserves inner products and entangled-state symmetries.
- For suitable bipartite entangled states, local unitary “envariance” swaps can leave the global state invariant while acting nontrivially on a subsystem (Zurek-style observation as a symmetry fact about some states).

### Added structure or idealizations (everything else)
- Selecting the measurement / pre-measurement scenarios in which envariance applies as the probability setup.
- Identifying envariant probabilities with Everettian branch credences / Born weights for agents.
- Assuming decohered branches / outcomes are already available as the bearers of those probabilities (**A1 dependency**).
- Principles linking symmetry → unique rational credence assignment (epistemic bridge principles).
- Extending from special symmetric cases to general Born rule for arbitrary amplitudes.

**Claim of E4:** Envariance symmetries of unitary entangled states force Born weights; hence A2 closes with no extra epistemic posits.

## 3. Red-team pass

Strongest objections:
1. **A1 circularity:** Probabilities need outcomes/branches; envariance arguments typically sit after or assume a measurement-like decomposition (E4.2).
2. **Silent epistemic posits:** E4.3 is exactly an extra epistemic principle.
3. **Redefinition:** E4.5 tries to win A2 by exempting the bridge from “epistemic postulate.”
4. **Scope gap:** E4.4 — moving from symmetric cases to full Born rule is nontrivial and contested.
5. **Literature status:** Envariance/Born programs remain disputed; L4b already marked A2 intractable to close under consensus.
