## 1. Plain-language summary

Ed Zitron’s Aug 11–12 writing says Nvidia’s ~$500B Wall Street financing push is effectively **vendor/circular financing**, still largely **theoretical (MoUs)**, and is being used to support extraordinary GPU-sale expectations while buyers take on huge debt. He also argues OpenAI and Anthropic dominate hyperscaler **AI** revenue (~70%+) and that scaled demand is not sustainable without those two labs’ fundraising.

**What held up:** Nvidia **did announce** MoUs with six firms aiming to mobilize over $500 billion. **Right now** that figure is MoU/aim language — not closed funded capital or booked Nvidia revenue. We froze what “vendor/circular,” “70% concentration,” and “unsustainable demand” **mean**. We locked Zitron’s Nvidia FY/~$1.6T table and his 70% **citations** as *what he claims*. Independently, FY26 revenue is **$215.9B reported**, and his FY27–29 **total-revenue** table is **near current Street averages**. Microsoft’s 10-K discloses **$24.1B** FY26 related-party revenue from OpenAI.

**What did not hold up as proven:** That the package **is** vendor financing; that Nvidia will sell **>$1.6T of GPUs** (Street FY27–29 **company revenue** sum is a different quantity); that customers’ “hundreds of billions” of debt is a consensus print; that OpenAI+Anthropic are ≥70% of Microsoft/Google/Amazon **AI** revenue (Bloomberg’s ~70% is a Microsoft-only estimate with an extrapolated AI total; Google’s cited 48% is **Cloud**, not AI); that demand is a “bubble” under the sustainability bar (capex is large and some non-lab AI products exist — that is not the elevation).

**Bottom line:** The announcement and present-tense MoU status are solid. The economic indictment is well-posed and **not cleared**. This is not investment advice. CNBC Squawk was **kept out** of the claim package.
