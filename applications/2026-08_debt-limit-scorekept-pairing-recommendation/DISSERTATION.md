## 1. Plain-language summary

This application tested a **revised** claim: that debt-limit increases or suspensions **should** be procedurally paired with official CBO-style scored non-interest spending cuts, over the budget window, with anti-gimmick rules, at least equal to the borrowing headroom the limit action creates.

What held up is the **descriptive core**. The embedded scorekeeping rule is a well-posed balance test. Debt-limit action remains financing permission, not a new spending appropriation. On the named 2023 Fiscal Responsibility Act episode, scored cuts were far smaller than headroom: the episode **fails** the numerical standard.

What did **not** hold up is the soft recommendation itself. Well-posedness does not force “should.” A quantitative follow-on showed that meeting the standard for FRA would have required about **3.6×** the actual cut package (~$4.7T vs ~$1.31T). Even in that counterfactual, only a **stipulated** scorekept “balanced” label follows by construction — not unrestricted fiscal responsibility, and not proof that Congress ought to adopt the rule.

**Bottom line:** Descriptive pairing criterion constrained; FRA fails; soft “should” left open and not pursued. Hard stop as **Stable Provisional**. Parent forced-deviation findings stay on the parent record.
