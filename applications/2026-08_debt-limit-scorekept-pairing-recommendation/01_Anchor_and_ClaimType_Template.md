## L₀ — Objective Anchors

1. The United States has a statutory federal debt limit (ceiling); Congress periodically raises or suspends it so the Treasury can continue to meet already-obligated payments.
2. Raising or suspending the debt limit does not, by itself, authorize new spending programs; it permits financing of obligations already incurred under prior law (standard institutional description).
3. Federal budget scorekeeping uses baselines, windows (e.g. 10-year), and definitions of “spending” / “cuts” that are contested in practice (CBO-style baselines, current-law vs current-policy, mandatory vs discretionary, interest, etc.).
4. Soft policy recommendations (“should”) are normative unless derived from stipulated criteria plus admitted premises; they are not forced by scorekeeping definitions alone.
5. Macroeconomic effects of spending paths, tax paths, and debt trajectories—and default/disruption risks if debt-limit action is delayed—depend on timing, composition, and conditions, not on a pairing slogan alone.
